# The Girl at the River — story plan, 2026-10-24

Built from Sami's pitch by a five-person team: story architect, live-stream story researcher, character/dialogue writer, systems designer, critic. Where they disagreed, the options are laid out under **Decisions for Sami**.

## Logline

A wounded father brings his small daughter to a town that has everything. The town's leader smiles and promises them a home "next age", and keeps them waiting through six ages. She grows up, buries her father, and takes the town from him.

**Theme:** who the town belongs to: the people who build it, or the one who cuts the ribbon. The game already says this every age when the head declares the Wonder chat built "open". The story just makes Ama notice.

**The spine:** he gathers power and she gathers people. His title climbs through six kinds of authority (Chief, High Priest, King, Alderman, Mayor, Overseer). She learns the counter to each: grief, then law, labour, the vote, and finally just standing together.

---

## What everyone agreed on

- **Daughter, not son.** It's already built (Ama, her lines, her growth each age), and "the small girl the Chief dismissed becomes the leader" reads instantly. She also looks clearly different from Sami's figure in every shot.
- **Sami's Chief is a comic heel, never cruel.** He's vain, he takes credit, and he's absent when it counts. He mocks builds, never people, and always helps a brand-new chatter first. Being careless with other people's things is enough to deserve the overthrow without becoming a monster.
- **The secrecy is dramatic irony, not real secrecy.** Ama whispers to chatters in a distinct bubble. Everyone can see it, and the Chief "doesn't". If Sami walks close, she cuts off mid-line ("…lovely river today, Chief."). This becomes a running gag: Sami can hunt for her whispering.
- **Chat takes sides by doing things, not by voting.** Sides show as a mark on the tag, and **red is Ama's colour** (the writer, critic and researcher all landed on it independently). Changing sides is announced on stream.
- **Grievances come from Sami's own play.** Every time he fines, taxes, KOs someone, outbuilds someone or neglects Toma, it's logged automatically. Ama reads the list at the climax, so the more of a heel Sami was, the stronger her case. His improv writes the ending, so it can't feel scripted.
- **Fixed scenes, live improv in between** (the Dream SMP model). Scenes decide *what happens*; Sami decides *who the Chief is* while it happens.
- **Episodes.** Each stream has one big beat and ends on a cliffhanger, opens with a "Previously…" card, and the two big ones are announced ahead as event streams.
- **What has to change in the code:** today each age gets a new travellers' leader, Toma only exists in the Stone Age, and Ama's Medieval line "My father is gone now" kills him off screen. Toma and Ama have to persist across ages so the ageing and the death happen on camera.

**The secret: the Great Hunt** (the writer's version, recommended over "the Chief cut the bridge"):
- Toma's wound came from the night the Chief's hunters drove a mammoth herd into the river for glory. The herd ran through the band's camp and took the bridge with it.
- Sami tells the Great Hunt as pure comedy from minute one, so chat hears it ten times. Every brag then becomes evidence.
- There are two steps to the reveal:
  1. Toma's deathbed hint: "The herd was driven. I saw feathers on the hunter's head."
  2. The Industrial dredging finds hunt spearheads and the Chief's feathered headdress in the mud of the old crossing.
- Sami decides live how the Chief answers: deny it, shrug ("different times"), or "…that was your father?" The writer recommends playing it as genuinely not knowing.

**The recurring cast** (travels with the family every age):
- **Old Ura:** deadpan elder who never dies ("Still here. Don't ask."). She saw the hunt and drops hints.
- **Bo:** Toma's big, gentle friend. He carries Toma across the bridge, carves the gravestone, and stands behind Ama at the end.
- **Tusk:** a boy who worships the Chief and rises into his service, ending as guard captain. Whether he lowers his weapon is the turning point of the overthrow, and chat can sway him.

**Sami's constants in every age:**
- He calls her **"small one"**.
- He always builds the grander version.
- He tells the Great Hunt story.
- He has **a hat he won't give up**.

All four pay off in the overthrow.

## The big scenes (condensed; full scripts in the writer's notes)

**The death.** Bronze turn, about 25 s, played *during* the party:
1. The High Priest: "I declare the Keep open!" Confetti, cheering.
2. Cut across the river to the camp at dusk, the fireworks reflected in the water.
3. Toma: "Look. Look at it."
4. Toma: "Ama. The bridge didn't fall by itself." / "Carry it like a lamp, not a stone." / "Live on the other side."
5. Ama: "Father?" Three seconds of silence while the fireworks keep going.
6. Black, then the title card **THE MEDIEVAL AGE**.

Chat's care changes how it plays. A high health bar means he walks to the square to see the Wonder first and names his top carer. If Sami sat with him, Ama says "Thank the Chief for coming." If not, she says "He never came", and that becomes a grievance. Weapons are locked for 30 s (there's already an admin switch for this).

**The grave.** Medieval opening, about 20 s. The camera rises from grown Ama kneeling at a bare mound and pulls out across the river to the medieval town. She says "My father is gone now" (already in the data). Afterwards chatters can "leave flowers at the grave", and the crier posts "@X left flowers for Toma."

**The Rising.** The Wasteland climax, about 7 minutes:
1. Ama climbs the rocket pad and reads the top grievances, then: "Who stands with me?" Title card: **THE RISING**.
2. **Muster, 60 s.** Dudes with a red mark walk to her, the Chief's people walk to him, and anyone can join with `!rise` / `!hold`. That's one word, no model calls, and it works for a 100-person chat.
3. **The struggle, about 4 min.** A square-control meter moves with the bodies standing in the ring, and knockouts swing it. **Sami fights as the boss** with an Authority health bar (combat code already has an unused boss slot). Pilots on /play are the swing votes. Sami can also flip wavering Ama supporters by naming them, so he has to choose between holding the ring and chasing people.
4. **The scene:**
   - Ama: "Everyone can hear me. That's new for me."
   - Ama: "You drove the herd into the river. My father was on the bank."
   - Chief: "…That was your father?"
   - Ama: "You never asked. You never ask."
   - Tusk lowers his weapon and walks to the red crowd.
   - Chief: "Fine. Fine. But I'm keeping the hat."
   - Ama: "Keep the hat. Give me the key."
   - Chief: "…Small one."
   - Ama: "Not any more."
   - Optionally, Sami types the Chief's last words live. She throws the keys in the river.
5. **Epilogue.** The Old Chief, sweeping the square: "Did I ever tell you about my Great Hunt?" Ura: "Yes. Every age."

## How chat takes part

- **Sides:** one number per chatter, from the Chief's side (−) to Ama's (+). It fades a little each age so newcomers can still tip things. It moves through actions:

  | Moves toward Ama | Moves toward the Chief |
  |---|---|
  | Shifts on her asks | Meeting his decrees |
  | Doing her whispered errands | Taking his gifts and bribes |
  | Being on the receiving end of a grievance | Being named in his speeches |
  | Saying "I stand with Ama" (`pledge`, dude kneels, visible) | Saying "I stand with the Chief" |

- **Whispers** start in Medieval. Every ~3 min Ama walks to one dude and says a template line filled from the grievances ("He fined you §15 for wearing blue."), plus a small errand the game can check ("If you're with me, take flowers to the grave."). She stops if the Chief comes within 12 m, and if he catches her the crier calls it.
- **Forks** run Medieval to Modern. Some visits offer two rival asks, the Chief's and Ama's, and the first one finished wins. The losing petitioner leaves for that age, so the cost is permanent and remembered.
- **The Ledger:** named deeds, recorded from templates. It tracks who laid the bridge's last plank, who sat with Toma, who carried the first stone, the first to defect, and who stood first at the Rising. Ama quotes it ("@sara mended the bridge when I was small.").
- **Sami's levers** (new operator buttons):
  - **Decree:** wear my colour, everyone to the square, a feast in my honour, pay the tithe.
  - **Tax** 0/10/20%, which fills his treasury.
  - **Give** from the treasury, i.e. bribes.
  - **Gift build** on someone's lot.
  - **Promise ledger:** he logs each promise he improvises, and she reads them back.
  - **Concede:** he can always throw the climax for the story.

  Every lever wins loyalty *and* feeds her grievances: power costs grievances.
- **On stream, kept phone-light:**
  - A red or gold mark on tags.
  - A split bar under the board: `AMA 7 ▮▮▮▮▮▮▮░░░░░ 5 CHIEF · 9 undecided`.
  - Her top 3 grievances.
  - Sided dudes drift to their corner of the square, so the wide shot shows the split.

## Running it on stream

**Four episodes** (6–12 h at measured pace; the first two may each need two streams):

1. **The River.** Stone, maybe into Bronze. Opens with 15–20 min of free building and the Chief bullying people, then the river cutscene.
2. **The Last Winter**, an announced event. Bronze: sickness, the healer race, the death. Ends on the title card.
3. **The Decree.** Medieval to Modern: whispers, forks, the reveal, the election, the collapse.
4. **The Reckoning**, an announced event. Wasteland: the mirror opening, the Rising, the finale.

Each stream:
- Opens with a 60–90 s **"Previously…"** card: three Ledger lines, two replayed shots, "Tonight:".
- Shows a 10 s "So far tonight" card every ~30 min.
- Ends with a cliffhanger card: "Next time: The Reckoning, Sat 20:00." Sami says it in character.

**The newcomer arriving in age 4 needs to be hooked in 60 seconds:**
- A persistent one-line strip: `Age 4 · Toma is dead · Ama vs the Mayor · 14 with her · 9 with him`.
- Ama meets them at the bus stop: "New here? The Mayor says this town is his. I say it's ours. Where do you stand?"
- One starter errand puts them in the Ledger in their first minute.
- `!story` gives two lines.
- The best hook is Sami trying to bribe them.

**Clip moments**, each about 45 s or less with context on screen in the first 3 s:
- The Chief building a bigger house next to yours.
- The last plank.
- Ama going quiet as the Chief walks up.
- The candle and flowers count at the grave.
- A loud loyalist switching sides.
- The rigged count.
- "Keep the hat. Give me the key."
- The exile shot.

**Example titles:**
- "My chat is plotting to overthrow me (I can see it)"
- "If you don't build a hospital, he dies"
- "Tonight I lose the throne… unless"

## Running Ama cheaply

- **Scenes, whispers, greetings, the near-Chief cut-off and story beats** are templates, with zero model calls.
- **Free conversation** only happens when a chatter names her or speaks within reach. It runs on the fast model with an age-gated persona, roughly 40 calls an hour and a 60 s cooldown per chatter. The full persona block is in the writer's notes.
- **What she knows is gated by age.** She doesn't know the secret until the deathbed, she never says "overthrow", and she never mentions the stream.
- **Other characters can't advance the story.** Story characters can't be KO'd outside the Rising, and agents can't heal Toma or crown Ama. Only scenes change story state.
- **Added cost per run:** about 4–5 extra design calls (under $1), whispers capped at about 20 agent turns an hour, and one nudge per fighter at the Rising.

## Decisions for Sami

1. **Can the overthrow fail?**
   - **(a)** She always wins; chat's sides decide *how* (peaceful crowd, siege, size of the crowd). This is your pitch.
   - **(b)** It's a real contest. If the Chief wins, she's exiled across the river and **the bridge breaks behind her**, mirroring the opening: "You'll need a bridge again someday." It has a Concede button so you can still throw it.

   The systems designer and critic lean **(b)** for stakes and replay; the researcher leans **(a)**. My take: **(b)**. It's what makes your villainy matter, and you keep Concede.
2. **When does Toma die?**
   - At the **Bronze→Medieval** turn (architect): it closes an early stream, and the existing Medieval line "My father is gone now" already fits.
   - At **Medieval→Industrial** (writer): one more age of him declining.

   Recommend **Bronze→Medieval**.
3. **Where does the family live?**
   - **(a)** In town as residents (systems: easiest for whispers).
   - **(b)** At a camp across the river that **never changes style** while the town modernises (architect: the broken promise you can see from a phone).

   Recommend a hybrid: Toma stays at the frozen camp, and Ama crosses into town every day.
4. **What's the secret?**
   - **The Great Hunt**, recommended: comic seed, deathbed hint, Industrial proof.
   - **The Chief cut the bridge on purpose**: darker, makes you a real villain.
5. **His fate after the overthrow:** exiled across the river (sitting by Toma's cairn), or stays as the Old Chief and you keep playing him humbled. Possibly chat's call.
6. **The next run:** the Wasteland settles to dust and dust is the Stone Age ("Rivers move. Bridges fall."). The stranded elder in the new opening wears **the Overseer's goggles**, or is **Old Ama**.
7. **Names:** keep Ama and Toma, or let chat name the daughter in stream 1 (there's already a rename control).

## Alternative twists for later runs

- **The Trial:** loyalists can save the Chief. This is decision 1(b) as a whole run.
- **Ama becomes the worse ruler.** She takes over in Modern, the Wasteland is *her* regime, and the deposed Chief leads the counter-revolt toward a reconciliation at the rocket.
- **The Chief cut the bridge.** Rotate it in on run 2 once the Great Hunt is known.
