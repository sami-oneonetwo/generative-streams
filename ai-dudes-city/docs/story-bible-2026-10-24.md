# FINDERS KEEPERS: the story bible

**2026-10-24.** *AI Dudes: the town.* Final head-writer pass, after the story, chatter-POV, operator-load, feasibility and cinematic critics.

**This supersedes `docs/story-2026-10-24.md` wherever the two conflict.** Sami's brief (see `docs/story-handoff-2026-10-24.md` and the 2026-10-24 brief) overrides both.

---

## 1. Pitch, theme, title

**Pitch.** The Chief turns up at a camp while its owners are away and decides it's his. He pulls the logs out of their bridge to finish his hut: "Free wood!" That evening the owners come home. They are a hurt father, his daughter Wren and their small band, back from a long, hard expedition. They find their way home broken and a stranger waving at them from their own fire.

Over six ages, one per stream, the head keeps building monuments to himself on other people's ground. Wren keeps count. Chat lives in the middle. Every brick a chatter lays goes to one of the two. In the last age Wren asks the town to stand with her, and the town really decides whether he falls.

**Theme.** *He builds himself out of other people's homes.*

**Chat's role in one line, the same in every age:** *Every brick goes to someone. Who did you build for?*

**Working title.** *Finders Keepers.* It's his motto in the first scene. If he wins, it's her threat in the last one.

**Two rules hold the whole thing up:**
1. **The head is never scripted to be cruel.** His selfishness lives entirely in *what he builds*. Sami plays a vain idiot, not a villain, and his comedy stays his own.
2. **Chat always knows more than Wren.** Chat watched him break the bridge. Wren only finds out in the Medieval age. Until then, every "The bridge was like that when I got here" is a joke the audience is in on.

## 2. The cast

The names are plain old words. They fit a stone hut and a scrap convoy equally well, and they're 3–4 letters with different first letters, so they read on a phone tag and don't blur on mic.

Each cast tag carries a fixed role so the words never get confused with ordinary chat: **Wren**, **Ash the hunter**, **Old Moss**, **Oak the builder**, **Kit**. Cast names are never commands (the commands are `red` and `gold`, see section 4).

| Name | Why the name | Who they are | How they drive the story | Look that survives every restyle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wren** | A tiny bird famous for being loud for its size. | The daughter. A teenager in Stone, a woman by Medieval, the town's leader at the end. | She leads every rally, asks the head for help in Bronze, learns the truth in Medieval, refuses his bribe in Industrial and calls the Rising. | **Dark red.** A cord in her hair becomes a band, a hood, a scarf, a jacket and finally a bandana. Her red hood is the biggest shape in any frame she's in. A bone whistle opens every rally. |
| **Ash** | He burns down. Ash falls again in the Wasteland. | The father and expedition leader, wounded in the leg. | He sets the first ask ("Mend the bridge"). His wound opens the story and his death turns it. | Tall, grey-streaked, bandaged left leg, carved staff. A red cord on his wrist, which Wren takes when he dies. |
| **Old Moss** | Old, quiet, outlasts everything. | The elder. **She saw the Chief pull the logs and kept quiet**, because Ash wanted peace. | She's the recap: a 30-second "Previously…" each stream. She reveals the truth in Medieval and reads the Follies at the trial. She never dies. | Green shawl, always sitting on something, a knotted cord in her lap. She ties one knot per Folly. |
| **Oak the builder** | Big, steady, built to last. | Gentle and huge. | He stands on Wren's site and calls the builds. In Medieval he refuses to haul for the King, the band's first "no". In Industrial he walks off the line, which exposes Wren's rally. | Orange apron, mallet, tool belt. |
| **Kit** | Small and new. | The band's boy, who worships the head. | **He's chat's mirror and the head's sidekick.** From the Crossing on, he follows the head around, copies his poses and cheers his nonsense. At the Rising his step decides the final shot. | Small, plain blue, gold badge. |

**Signature lines** (short and speakable):
- **Wren:** "That was our fire." / "You built that. Not him." / "Don't shout at him. Just build ours." / "Not any more." / "Keep the hat. Give me the key."
- **Ash:** "We were gone one winter. One." / "Mend the bridge, and we'll mend the rest."
- **Moss:** "Previously…" / "Hm." / "I was there." / "Still here. Don't ask."
- **Oak:** "Planks here. I'll show you." / "I don't argue. I build."

### The head's constants

These are Sami's toolkit, not a script. Each one pays off at the end.

- **Gold.** His side's colour, his sash, his hat.
- **The hat grows every age.** He never takes it off.
- **"Trust me. It's going to be amazing."** He says it about every cordon. Wren's payoff: "It was amazing. For you."
- **"The bridge was like that when I got here."** Moss says "Hm." every time until Medieval. Then she says "I was there."
- **"Small one."** His name for Wren. Her payoff: "Not any more."
- **"...Still fine."** He says it when the bridge falls in Stone and when the tower blows in Modern.
- **He cuts the ribbon** on every Wonder that chat built.

His title by age: Chief, High Priest, King, Alderman, Mayor, Overseer.

## 3. The full story, age by age

**Every age has the same shape:**
- One stream.
- **The Board** shows his Folly in gold and Wren's job in red.
- **One Unveiling** of his Folly, ending on a Wren pivot that changes every age.
- **One or two Rallies.**
- **One story beat**, in which a cast member's action causes the next thing.
- The Wonder push, then the **age turn**, then a **Verdict** and a cliffhanger.

**The Folly stays standing**, unrestyled, just like Wonders already do. By the end, the town is full of evidence against him.

**Who drives each age:**

| Age | Cast member | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Stone | Ash | Sets the bridge ask |
| Bronze | Wren | Asks the head for help |
| Medieval | Moss | Tells Wren who broke the bridge |
| Industrial | Oak | His walk-off exposes the rally |
| Modern | Kit | Takes off his badge, or pins it back on |
| Wasteland | Wren | Calls the Rising |

### The Intro: "Finders Keepers" (Stone, about 70 s, Sami's GO)

The town is live and chatters are building. At the east edge, by the river, stands the band's old camp: a ring of huts, a cold hearth, and a carved post with a red handprint, buried under the Chief's junk. The bridge is drawn intact until this scene.

1. **Title card** over a wide of the town: *AI DUDES: THE TOWN.*
2. **Card: "EARLIER TODAY."** The flashback is graded sepia. A wide of the camp, empty, morning. The log bridge stands.
3. **Low shot from the hill.** The Chief walks in. This is a scripted stand-in townie in `MAYOR_LOOK`, and Sami's live Mayor is hidden for the flashback. *Chief:* "Ooh. Free huts." He plants a stick flag in their hearth. "Mine now."
4. **Side-on at the bridge.** His hut is one log short. He yanks a log out. *Chief:* "Free wood!" Creak. "It's fine." He yanks another, and the bridge folds into the river with a splash (the `collapse` beat). A beat. "...Still fine."
5. **Hard cut. Card: "NOW."** Colour returns. On the far bank, figures stagger out of the trees: Ash limping and leaning on Oak, Wren in front, Moss behind, Kit trailing.
6. **Close on Wren:** "We're home."
7. **Over Wren's shoulder** across the river: no bridge, smoke from their hearth, a flag on it, and **Sami's live Mayor standing at their fire**. The server puts him there at Call.
8. **Mayor slot.** Cut to Sami, held for as long as he holds Tab. His mic is the line. The cue card offers "Hello! Welcome to my town!" *Kit, tiny, waving back:* "He waved at me!"
9. **Close on Ash:** "Wren. That's our fire."
10. **Wide of the river between the banks.** *Moss:* "Well. Somebody fix it, then."
11. **Two big panels, held 8 s:** red *MEND THE BRIDGE: type red*, and gold *HELP THE CHIEF: type gold*. Then play opens. The Chief's cordon goes up on its fixed plot by the camp, with the sign "KEEP OUT. SOMETHING AMAZING."

Ash's satchel holds the red stone (copper) the expedition went to find. It becomes the Furnace.

### Age 1, Stone: the Chief and **The Big Hut**

- **RED:** *Mend the bridge.* This uses the existing shift mechanic. After the crossing, the job becomes *Make room: build the band a hut on your lot*. Wren: "You're on our land too. Make room." Any hut you build counts. Choosing red means giving something back.
- **GOLD:** *The Big Hut*, on the camp plot where their settlement stood.
- **Story beat: the Crossing** (this exists). Ash limps over on Oak's shoulder. *Chief:* "Mind the floor. It's new." Kit runs straight to the Chief, and from here on he follows him around.
- **Unveiling.** A huge hut with a tiny door. The band's red-handprint post is his doorpost, and their hearth stones are his doormat. *Chief:* "Behold. A hut. For me."
- **Wren pivot.** She's at the riverbank. If the bridge isn't mended yet, she's on the *far* bank, which is sadder. *Wren:* "That was our fire."
- **Wren's stage: doubt.** Her rallies are small and almost kind: "He's not wicked. He's just not serious. Watch him."
- **Turn.** The Furnace is built from Ash's red stone. The Chief cuts the ribbon: "My idea!"
- **Cliffhanger.** Ash lies by the fire and doesn't get up. *Wren:* "He'll be better by the bronze days." *Moss:* "Hm."

### Age 2, Bronze: the High Priest and **The Golden Me**

- **RED:** *A healer's hut for Ash*, the first one built near the camp. Then *grow herbs*: any garden counts.
- **GOLD:** *A 20 m statue of himself*, poured from the first bronze, made from Ash's red stone.

**Story beat: The Ask** (early in the age, a small in-place scene, Sami's GO)
- Wren walks up to the Priest wherever he is. Close two-shot. *Wren:* "He's worse. We need the bronze for medicine."
- **Mayor slot.** Sami answers live. The cue card gives three lines, and pressing 1, 2 or 3 makes one the subtitle:
  1. "After the statue, small one."
  2. "Have you tried walking it off?"
  3. "...Take what you need." **This one helps.**
- **This is Sami's choice, and it counts.**
  - If he helps (line 3, or he says so and presses 3), the healer's hut jumps 50%, and Ash will mention it as he dies. It also opens the door for ending B.
  - Otherwise the neglect is *his*, done on camera, and "You let him die" will be true.
  - Either way Ash dies. The death is the hinge of the story.

**Unveiling.** The statue rises with a fanfare.
- **Pivot:** not to Wren in the distance. Over Wren's shoulder from the healer's-hut doorway (or the cold hearth, if the hut isn't built), with Ash's bedroll behind her and the statue glinting beyond.
- *Ash, off-screen:* "Is it nice?" *Wren:* "No."

**Wren's stage: grief.**

**Turn: The Last Light** (section 6). This is the most important scene of the run.
- The Keep ribbon is cut and the Priest gives his speech (Mayor slot). Then a hard cut to Ash.
  - **If chat finished the healer's hut**, he dies inside, warm, and gets all three lines: "It's warm. Who built this?" (naming the top builder, "Tell Kev thank you"), "Don't carry this like a stone, Wren," and "Live."
  - **If not**, he dies on a bedroll by the cold hearth and gets one line: "Live."
- Wren ties his red cord on her wrist. The age restyles *over the grave*. The camera pulls back from Wren, now older, standing at the grave in the new Medieval town.
- Then the candle window.

**Cliffhanger.** Card: *SHE WON'T FORGET.*

### Age 3, Medieval: the King and **The Royal Moat**

- **RED:** *Ash's cairn*. Type `red` to carry a stone, and it grows with each helper. Then *a stone hall for the band*.
- **GOLD:** *A palace inside a moat* between the town and the camp.
- **Cast.** Sami may knight Kit live ("Sir Kit!"), and Kit gets a wooden sword. Oak refuses to haul for the King.
- **Story beat: The Knot** (a stream-only rally at the cairn).
  - *Moss:* "He pulled the logs. I watched."
  - *Wren:* "...You let me thank him?"
  - *Moss:* "Your father wanted peace. I'm done with peace."
  - From now on, every "The bridge was like that" gets a Moss "I was there."
- **Unveiling.** The drawbridge rises behind the King and cuts the camp off. **The front row of the crowd is trapped on the palace side until the release.** *King:* "A bridge that only opens for me. Genius."
- **Wren pivot**, from behind the raised drawbridge: "He broke our bridge. Now he owns one."
- **Wren's stage: defiance.** "He takes what you build and puts his name on it."
- **Turn.** The Steam Engine is built from Oak's design, and the King cuts the ribbon.
- **Cliffhanger.** Wren climbs the palace wall: "They built it. Not you." The King laughs. The crowd's bubbles say what they say.

### Age 4, Industrial: the Alderman and **The Bust Works**

- **RED:** *Workshops for the band*. Any workshop on your lot counts.
- **GOLD:** *A factory*.
- **Unveiling.** The conveyor starts and turns out little busts of him. **One bust lands on every chatter's lot.** *Alderman:* "One for every home. You're welcome."
- **Wren pivot.** She picks a bust off her own doorstep: "Now he's in our houses."
- **This is the first Folly that touches chatters' own lots**, so the neutrals get pushed to pick a side.
- **Story beat: He Finds Out** (armed, the run's midpoint and the first time Wren and the head face each other).
  1. Oak walks off the line. The Alderman's auto-walk follows him, straight into Wren's rally. *Wren:* "Act normal." Nobody does.
  2. **The Offer.** A town gathering at the Bust Works. The Alderman offers Wren a deputy sash and her own bust on the line. **Mayor slot, about 30 s**, for Sami to pitch it on mic.
  3. *Wren:* "Keep it. Like you kept our fire."
- **Wren's stage: organising.** Her rallies get bigger, and she names her builders one by one.
- **Turn.** The Power Station lights every window except the camp's.
- **Cliffhanger card:** *WREN IS STANDING AGAINST HIM.*

### Age 5, Modern: the Mayor and **The Members' Golf Course**

- **RED:** *A Wren sign on your lot*. Every red sign shows across town, so the map *is* the side tally.
- **GOLD:** *A golf course* over the riverbank and the camp.
- **Unveiling.** He drives out in a cart, **straight through the crowd ring**. The sign reads "MEMBERS: 1". *Mayor:* "Hole in one. Every time."
- **Wren pivot.** The camera moves past her to the 18th flag, planted on Ash's cairn. **She says nothing.** It's the strongest silence of the run.
- **Story beat: Kit's Badge.** Kit stands at the cairn. Then it depends on the tally:
  - If red leads, he takes the badge off: "He put a flag on Ash."
  - If gold leads, he holds it, shaking, and pins it back on: "...He's still the Boss."
- **Wren's stage: campaign.** Her rallies are still out of the Mayor's sight, but now they fill the square.
- **Turn: Still Fine** (armed). This mirrors the intro.
  1. At the Launch Tower ribbon, his clubhouse is one part short. He pulls a rod out of the tower: "Free power!" Creak. "It's fine." Boom. "...Still fine."
  2. Sirens, the lights go out, and the town rusts.
  3. He sprints into the clubhouse bunker and locks the hatch, leaving chat's dudes outside.
  4. Over Wren's shoulder: the sealed hatch. *Wren:* "Oak. Get the people out."
  
  The end of the world is his fault, and it's the biggest clip of the run.

### Age 6, Wasteland: the Overseer and **One Seat**

- **RED:** *The scrap rocket*, the existing Wonder. It's Wren's plan to get everyone out.
- **GOLD:** Early in the age, the Overseer climbs out of the bunker and cordons the rocket pad: "COMING SOON: MY ESCAPE." **His last Folly is the town's own Wonder.** He puts his name on what chat built. That's the theme at full size, and it needs no extra build.
- **Cast.**
  - Moss's "Previously" this stream is Ash's last words.
  - Kit is captain of the guard.
- **No Wren pivot this age.** She walks *into* the shot. The watching is over.
- **The final Unveiling becomes the Rising** (section 6). He unveils the finished rocket: one seat, with his name on it. *Overseer:* "One seat. Mine." Everyone is already gathered. *Wren:* "Who stands with me?"

**The three endings.** The count is honest and shown live. Ties go to Wren.

- **A. Overthrown.**
  - Red has more heads standing with Wren. Kit steps aside: "Sorry, Boss. I'm with them."
  - *Wren:* "Keep the hat. Give me the key."
  - The rocket launches with the town aboard. The last shot is the Overseer buckled into the back row. *Wren:* "There's a seat for you. At the back."
- **B. Abdication.** Sami's choice, at any time during the Rising, by holding B for 2 s.
  - He hands her the key himself. *Wren:* "...Huh." *Moss:* "Still here."
  - If he helped in the Bronze Ask, Wren adds: "He gave the bronze. I remember that too."
  - It's the redemption ending, and all of it is his improv.
- **C. He holds.**
  - Gold has more heads standing. Kit stays. The Overseer launches alone.
  - Wren leads the band back over the old bridge. Then, over her shoulder, *she* pulls the logs out. *Wren:* "Next time, we'll be here first." Card: *FINDERS KEEPERS.*
  - The next run opens on a camp with a red handprint on its post. Failure becomes the season-two hook, not a dead end.

## 4. Chat's role

### The four core mechanics. Everything else is dropped.

**1. The Board.** It replaces the caravan board (`renderCaravan`), sits always on screen in free play, and looks the same in every age. It shows two thick bars, one word each, with the command under each:

```
  ████████░░  BIG HUT        ██░░░░  BRIDGE 3/8
     type gold                  type red
  ══════════ GOLD 7 ═════╪═══ RED 4 ══════════
```

- **The bars.** The left (gold) bar is his Folly and the right (red) bar is Wren's job. One word each. Text is never under 54 px.
- **The tug line** underneath is the side tally across awake chatters, gold against red.
- **The ticker.** One small rotating line under the board changes every 20 s and is hidden during scenes. It shows the premise ("The Chief took Wren's home"), the latest turncoat ("Kev crossed to red") and joiners ("Nia joined gold").
- **The Wonder.** In the last 30 minutes the two bars merge into one, **THE WONDER**. It fills red and gold by who put in what. The head cuts the ribbon anyway.

**2. Sides: red and gold**
- **Two fixed words for the whole run: `red` and `gold`.** The words never change with the age's title.
  - Typing one joins that side *and* sends your dude to help with that side's job.
  - Once you're on a side, a bare `help` works your side's job.
  - A neutral's `help` goes to Wren's job.
  - All of this runs on the keyword fast path, not the AI.
- **Markers you can read on a phone.** A coloured ground disc under the dude, plus a name-tag icon: a crown for gold, a flag for red. Dark red and bright gold differ in brightness as well as colour, so the sides survive colourblindness. The wrist cord and badge are flavour on top.
- **Switching** is allowed once per age, and it's always announced on the ticker. During the **Defection Window**, which opens automatically for 3 minutes after every Unveiling, switching is free and every crossing pops up live. Turncoats are content.
- **The Verdict.** At every age turn, the side that leads the tally changes the world, visibly and permanently:
  - **Red leads:** the band paints the Folly red, Kit takes the badge off, and Wren's next build gets the plaza spot.
  - **Gold leads:** the Folly grows a gold wing, and Kit polishes his badge.
  - Either way, the age's ending card shows it: *VERDICT: RED*.

**What each side gets:**

| | Gold (comfort) | Red (meaning) |
|---|---|---|
| **Gets** | Front row at Unveilings. **Your name on the Folly's plaque**, which Sami reads out triumphantly. His live "Friend of the Boss" call-outs. Wages for Folly shifts, from Bronze on. A "Friends of the Boss" roll call at the Rising, cheered by Sami. | **The rally secret:** Wren tells her people what's under the next tarp, and you get to spoil it in chat. Wren says your name. Your name goes on Wren's builds. You get to play *against the streamer*. |
| **Costs** | Your Folly is the evidence at the trial. The *Follies* are on trial, never the people. | No wages, never front row. |

Sami says it on mic, often: **"Nobody's overthrowing ME."** That's the invitation.

**3. The Bell.** This is free play interrupted by a call, a gathering, a scene and a release. There are two kinds of gathering:

- **Town gathering (forced, rare, an event):** the Intro, the Unveiling, the story beats that face the head, and the age turn.
- **Rally (opt-in):**
  - Wren's whistle sounds and a banner appears: **"WREN'S FIRE · type coming"**.
  - Only the dudes who reply are pulled in, and cast and extras fill the arc. Anyone can come, and a gold spy shows up wearing a crown in Wren's circle. That's fun.
  - Gold has something to do too. Typing **`warn`** during a rally pings /mayor ("3 warn: Wren's fire, NE"). Sami *may* walk over and scatter it. That turns "SAMI BEHIND YOU" pantomime into a real move, and red answers with "he's coming!"

**4. Crowd Mic.** In every gathering, the crowd's own words are the show, and prompts with one-word answers turn watching into typing. Answers become **pictures, not text**:

| Scene | Prompt | What it turns into |
|---|---|---|
| Unveiling | "RATE IT 1–10" | A big live average that every vote moves. Sami reacts: "A three?! Treason." |
| Rally | "Him or us?" | Each "us" lights a red pip at the fire and joins red. |
| The Last Light | "Say his name" | Every line lights a candle at the grave. |
| Rising | "Stand with Wren or the Overseer?" | Your dude walks to that half. The heads are the count. |

**Dropped on purpose:**
- Sabotage and theft from the cordon.
- Debt, feuds, raids, fork votes, whispers, grievance tallies.
- Strikes, and rigged elections. Faked outcomes break trust.
- Crowd fights.
- Wages for red.
- Per-age command words.

### What chat builds: four lanes

1. **Free builds** on your own lot. They're always allowed and never blocked. This is the toy that always works.
2. **Red: Wren's job.** Almost every red job is *a free build of the asked kind*. "Build a hut", "a garden", "a workshop" or "a Wren sign" anywhere counts and credits you.
   - Only two red jobs are shifts: the bridge (it exists) and the cairn, where watching it grow is the payoff.
   - Gold works for him. Red builds for themselves and each other. That's the theme.
3. **Gold: his Folly.** Shifts at the cordon speed it up and, from Bronze on, pay wages. The Folly also fills on its own timer, so Sami's beat never waits on gold helpers.
4. **The Wonder**, shared, at the end of each age.

### How the job is SHOWN on a phone

- **The Board:** two words and two commands.
- **Two sites in the world:**
  - Oak stands on Wren's site under a big floating red sign with the count ("BRIDGE 3/8").
  - The Folly has a gold beam, a tarp and a "SOMETHING AMAZING" sign. The beam pulls autoFrame's eye.
  - Labels explain things to newcomers, like a floating "ASH · WREN'S DAD" over the cairn.
- **After every scene, a 6 s NOW card** (96 px or larger): the next job and the word to type. *Every scene ends by telling chat what to build.*
- **The crier.** Its join reply carries the whole premise: "@nia you're in! The Chief took Wren's home. Type red or gold." It repeats the premise and the current asks every 5 minutes.
- **Cast idle bits.** One scripted bubble about every 3 minutes keeps the story in frame during free play:
  - Wren at the cold hearth: "He sleeps in our hut."
  - Kit copying Sami's pose.
  - Oak glaring at the tarp: "That's our timber."
  - Moss: "Hm."

### A chatter's timeline

- **First 60 s.** Their line shows as a bubble at once. The dude spawns with a burst, and autoFrame holds on them for about 5 s. The crier @-replies with the premise and the two words. They get a free first build.
- **First 10 minutes.**
  - The first build lands, and the first visitor pays them.
  - **A cast member names them**, favouring new and quiet chatters:
    - Oak: "@kev, one more plank."
    - Kit: "@kev, the Boss is the best, right?"
  - They pick a colour, and the ticker announces it.
- **Per stream.**
  - A loop of about 25 minutes: free play, then a gathering, then the release.
  - At least one Crowd Mic moment and one Defection Window.
  - The Verdict, then a closing standings card: "Tonight: red 9, gold 6. Kev crossed twice."
- **Across the run.** Your name piles up on plaques or Wren's builds. The Verdicts stay visible in the world as red paint or gold wings. Kit's badge carries over, and Moss's knots keep the count.

### Where chat's choices change the story

1. **Stone:** whether the band has crossed before the Unveiling, which sets where Wren watches from. Also how many band huts chat made room for: the band sleeps in huts or in tents.
2. **Bronze:** whether the healer's hut is done. That decides how Ash dies, whether he gets his last words, and whose name he says.
3. **Every age:** the Verdict, which repaints the Folly or grows it a wing, and moves Kit's badge.
4. **Modern:** Kit's badge scene plays out the tally.
5. **The ending:** A or C by the honest count, or B on Sami's choice.

Sami's own choices also change the story: the Bronze Ask and ending B.

## 5. The rhythm

**Free play is the default.** Gatherings are interruptions.

**Rules:**
- A full-town gathering happens at most once every 20 minutes.
- Every big beat has at least 15 minutes of free play on each side.
- Hard caps on how long dudes are locked in a scene:

| Scene | Lock cap |
|---|---|
| Gathering walk | 25 s |
| Rally | 60 s |
| Unveiling | 120 s |
| The Last Light | 150 s |
| Rising | 4 min |

**Beat states: locked, armed, firing, done.**
- **Armed** means the beat's conditions are met. For example: the Folly is at 100%, the previous beat is done, 3+ chatters are awake, and no scene has run for 4+ minutes.
- When a beat is armed, /mayor's lamp pulses and names it: "GO = THE UNVEILING". There's no chime. **The stream sees nothing.**
- **Big beats never fire by themselves.** Each one waits for Sami's GO, so it lands when the audience is there.
- A thin crowd shows as a warning on the lamp, never a lock.
- **GO starts the 25 s Call, not the scene.** Pressing GO again (or Esc) in the first 5 s cancels it on stream as a gag: "...the Chief changed his mind."

**Rallies:**
- **Stream 1:** rallies are beats in the GO queue.
- **From stream 2, they're automatic.** No prompt and no veto, so Wren really does plot behind his back.
- The server fires one when all of these hold:
  - 2+ chatters are awake;
  - no scene has run for 10+ minutes;
  - no rally has run for 20 minutes;
  - the head is 30+ m from Wren's fire;
  - Sami hasn't typed in the last 20 s;
  - no camera focus mode is on.
- In Stone, rallies wait until the band has crossed. Before that, there's nobody to rally.

**One age, about 110 minutes, one stream:**

| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Free play. Sami says hi as himself. The Board posts both jobs. |

## 6. The scenes

**Global rules:**
- Never more than **two text layers** at once: the subtitle plus one other.
- Letterbox bars are at most 7% of the frame, with subtitles inside them.
- Nothing that has to be read is under 54 px. Cards are 96 px or more.
- Pans move under about 10% of the frame width per second.
- The camera holds still during every reveal, using a few large particles. This keeps the stream's compression from smearing it.
- Title cards are only for big beats.

### Gathering in and out (the Bell)

1. **Call** (T−25 s, free play continues).
   - A top banner: *GATHER: THE UNVEILING 0:25*, with a 4-word premise underneath ("The Chief's secret build").
   - A light beam marks the spot, the crier posts in Kick chat, and Sami says something if he likes.
   - The 25 s soaks up Kick's 3–15 s delay.
   - **The server prepares the head.** It unhides him, dismounts him, puts the weapon away, turns off free camera and auto-walks him to his mark. WASD stays locked until the release. Sami never has to find a mark.
2. **Walk** (about 20 s).
   - Every awake dude gets a `gather` visit: a tiered ring slot facing the anchor, at 1.8× walking speed.
   - Away dudes pad the crowd, as the party already does.
   - The camera shows **a high wide of everyone converging.** That's the trailer shot.
3. **Gather mode** (see section 9, item 1). From here until the release, chat lines become bubbles and Crowd Mic input, and keywords are parsed. **No agent turns.** Moves and builds queue.
4. **Start**, when 70% have arrived or after 25 s. Stragglers fade out and back in at their slots, and late joiners spawn at the back. The letterbox comes in over 0.9 s and the HUD fades.
5. **Exit.**
   - The last shot eases into the pose autoFrame would choose, and the letterbox goes out.
   - Then the **NOW card**.
   - Errands are released with a staggered idle so the dudes don't move off as one block.

### How crowd chat is shown (Crowd Mic)

Sami's image is the crowd's own bubbles behind him, so **the crowd shot uses in-world bubbles, not a lower third.**

- **Bubbles.**
  - In a `crowd` shot, the `cinema-crowd` class un-hides `#people`.
  - Bubbles are pinned to a fixed screen size and grow to about 1.6×.
  - The 4 newest show in full, favouring chatters who haven't been shown yet tonight.
  - Lines are raw, never rewritten by the AI, and capped at 60 characters. Kick emotes show as pictures.
- **Safety gate for amplified lines.** Lines that fail any check still show as normal small bubbles, as they do today. Only lines that pass all of these get blown up:
  1. A fast local blocklist that normalises leetspeak and spacing, and drops @mentions, digit runs, links and repeated characters.
  2. The LLM moderation check, **failing closed**: if it times out, the line isn't amplified.
  3. The chatter has had 3+ earlier lines pass tonight.
- **Prompt answers become pictures:** a number tile, pips or candles.
- **The lower third is used only once:** in the Rising's sides mode (two columns, gold left and red right).
- **Holds and Kick's delay.**
  - Prompts appear **at the reveal**, not after it.
  - Crowd shots hold a minimum of 20 s. They only start checking for "quiet" after 15 s, where quiet means less than one line every 5 s. The maximum is 45 s.
  - Sami can hold Tab to keep a shot longer, or tap Tab to move on.
  - **Thin-crowd rule:** if fewer than 2 lines arrive in the 10 s after the reveal, the crowd shot is skipped. Townsfolk react with emotes only (gasp, clap), never text, so nothing is faked.

## 7. How Sami drives it

### The keys (about 6 presses a stream)

These are caught in a **capture-phase listener that runs before the `typing()` check**, so they work even while the say box is focused. They don't clash with WASD, C, H, F, R or G, or with the browser. A Stream Deck or macro pad is recommended.

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| **`** (backtick) | **GO.** Starts the Call for the beat the lamp names. Press it again within 5 s to cancel, as a gag. |
| **Tab** | In a Mayor slot or held shot: tap to move on, hold to keep. |
| **1 / 2 / 3** | During a slot only, puts that cue line up as the subtitle. In the Bronze Ask, 3 means "help". Otherwise 1–8 stay poses. |
| **Esc, held 1 s** | **Panic.** Ends the scene, releases the dudes, and puts the camera back on autoFrame. |
| **B, held 2 s** | During the Rising only: abdicate (ending B). |

**Never on F-keys:** F5 reloads the page and F12 opens devtools.

**Mayor slots: his mic is the line.**
- A slot lasts as long as he holds Tab, and ends when he lets go.
- With no input, it times out to an optional cue line.
- Typed subtitles are optional. The 1/2/3 cue lines give phone viewers on mute a subtitle without Sami typing anything.

### Automatic (he never touches these)

- The Board, both jobs, the Folly's timer and the tallies.
- The cordon going up, the Defection Window and the Verdict.
- Rallies (from stream 2) and gathering in and out.
- Positioning the head for scenes, every camera move and the Crowd Mic.
- The crier, the ticker, cast idle bits, moderation and scene watchdogs.

He never picks participants, moves the stream camera during a scene, counts sides, walks to a mark, or types against a clock.

### The /mayor panel

1. **One line, always on:**
   `NEXT: Unveiling ● ARMED · red 7 gold 4 · 9 awake · AI ●`
   When something is locked, it says why ("folly 72%"). During a scene it shows: `Unveiling · shot 4/9 · 0:48`.
2. **The lamp names the beat.** It pulses amber when armed. There's no chime, which would be missed or leak through his headset.
3. **Cue cards** in large type near the centre-bottom, shown **only when armed or in a scene**. Three short lines, numbered 1–3, plus plaque names when he should read them. For the Unveiling: "Behold. Mine." / "A hut fit for a Chief. One Chief." / "You may look. Don't touch."
4. **The grey "ON STREAM" chip** during rallies, plus `warn` pings ("3 warn: Wren's fire, NE").
5. **Autopilot**, for a break. Rallies still fire, big beats stay frozen and the camera stays on autoFrame.
6. **The safety drawer**, closed by default. It holds AI pause, clear bubbles, cancel designs, and mute or send home a dude, by click.

## 8. Engagement and investment

1. **A named result in 10 seconds.** Your line shows, your dude appears, and the crier @-replies with the premise and two words. A cast member says your name within 10 minutes.
2. **Sami says names aloud at every beat.** He reads the plaque names, thanks the bridge-builders and calls out turncoats. In a small or mid-sized channel, a streamer greeting people by name is the bond that keeps them.
3. **Playing against the streamer.** Red's pull isn't policy. It's the fun of plotting against the host while he "doesn't know". The rally secret gives red inside knowledge to spoil.
4. **Dramatic irony, at two levels.** Chat watched him break the bridge, and Wren doesn't know until Medieval. Chat sees the rallies, and the head doesn't. Sami's only job is to stay oblivious.
5. **Chat is in every shot.** The Crowd Mic plus one-word prompts turn watching into typing, and the prompts become pictures, so nobody's input is invisible. Losing control of your dude never means losing your voice.
6. **Deeds stay visible.** The Verdict permanently repaints or extends each Folly. Your name stays on a plaque or a Wren build for the rest of the run and is read out at the end. People join a side over a mark and an in-joke, so give them the disc, the crown and the flag.
7. **Recaps for mid-stream joiners.**
   - The crier's join line.
   - The ticker.
   - A 4-word premise on every gathering banner.
   - Labels over story objects.
   - Moss's "Previously".
   - The trial montage at the end.
8. **A cliffhanger every stream.** Every age ends on an image or card that the next "Previously" opens on.

## Appendix C: Wren's own Kick account (Sami's idea, reviewed separately)

Sami floated this after the room started: Wren gets her own Kick account, she chats in the main channel, and at some point her own stream goes live with her point of view. He'd rather keep the game itself in the one main stream. A separate reviewer looked at it.

**Feasibility: all three parts work without a rebuild.**
- **Posting as Wren.** Kick's chat API accepts `type:"user"` plus `broadcaster_user_id`, so her token can post into Sami's channel. Today the server posts as the streamer (`src/kick/send.ts:80-83`). To change that:
  - Run a second `KickAuth` with its own token file. Today there's one file, `kick-tokens.json` (`src/kick/oauth.ts:30`).
  - Add a second local-only login route next to `src/server/http.ts:52-78`.
  - Add a second `KickSender`.
  - Put her username in `KICK_IGNORE_BOTS` so she never becomes a chatter dude.
- **Her view.** The stream camera already eases close onto any character by id (`client/dudes/main.ts:1134-1141`), so a `?follow=<her id>` page is cheap. The real cost is a second OBS instance and encode on Sami's machine.
- **Her channel's chat.** Kick user ids are global, so identities match for free (`kick:${sender.user_id}`, `webhook.ts:119`).
  - **Trap:** the webhook ignores `payload.broadcaster`. Subscribing her channel would make its chat drive dudes exactly like the main chat. Route on broadcaster first.
  - **Also:** `isChatSubscription` and `resubscribe` (`subscriptions.ts:23`) need to filter by broadcaster.
- **Rules.** Kick bans botting views and chat, and impersonation. A clearly labelled character account doesn't break either rule. Never use it to pad numbers. Hosting needs both channels live and is limited to about 2 a day.

**Staging.** Never run a second channel all the time: it splits concurrent viewers, and on a phone, switching between two streams is painful.
- **Stone to Medieval: her account chats in the main channel only.**
  - About 6–10 lines per stream, all from templates (no model calls).
  - Triggers: thanking builders by name, heckling Unveilings ("@sara built that. He held the scissors."), and echoing her rallies.
  - The Mayor "doesn't read chat." Sami ignores her red name the way he plays dumb about rallies.
- **Industrial: "pirate radio."** Her channel goes live for 5–8 minutes on her follow-cam, reading out his Follies.
- **Modern:** a short campaign stream during the age.
- **Wasteland:** she goes live before the Rising ("the convoy gathers"), then hosts into the main channel at the count.
- **What her channel's chat does:** almost nothing. A first message there counts as "seen at the fire," a small nudge toward red that the crier mentions without naming anyone. It never causes an agent turn or an action. All real play stays in the main stream.
